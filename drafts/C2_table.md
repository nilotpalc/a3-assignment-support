### C2. Five Forces through an AI lens

| Force | Is AI strengthening or weakening this force? | Are you on the favourable side of the shift? | Most important implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barriers to entry | Strengthening: incumbents hold machine and lubricant performance data that a new product-level entrant cannot quickly assemble [J] | Yes [J] | The barrier only protects us if that performance data is clean and usable [J] |
| Buyer power | Strengthening: conversational AI lets buyers research needs and self-diagnose, so they rely less on our representatives [J] | No [J] | Differentiate through customer-linked or bundled solutions, not the product alone [J] |
| Supplier power | Weakening: AI-enabled logistics and procurement intermediaries reduce dependence on traditional providers, though physical reliability still dominates [J] | Partly [J] | Transporters may withhold network data; those with strong data and integration could command a premium and raise our switching costs [J] |
| Threat of substitutes | Strengthening: the lubricant itself is not displaced, but AI diagnostic apps can substitute after-sales and product-linked services; a global peer names digital and service solutions as a growth area [E:E3] | Yes: our performance data and OEM approvals support better AI-enabled services than app-only entrants can offer [J] | Data quality, user experience and integration across customer touchpoints become core to solution design [J] |
| Competitive rivalry | Strengthening: AI makes insight generation cheaper across domains and channels, raising the minimum standard; a domestic peer is investing in CRM, planning and BI tools [E:E2] | No [J] | Advantage shifts to acting on insights quickly, not only producing them [J] |
