C4. Proprietary-data moat / flywheel test - 3 marks

Selected opportunity: P4, sector-matched value cases (Priority 1).

Does the opportunity generate proprietary data competitors cannot easily obtain? Yes. P4 draws on our own product technical literature and customer success stories. The technical reasons behind each customer outcome sit only in company databases and are guarded as trade secrets, so peers cannot rebuild them from public sources.

Does that data feed a closed-loop system that improves the AI with continued use? Not yet; yes by design. The proposed solution would capture SME feedback on low-confidence escalations and bring in performance-analysis data, so that technical knowledge and sector insights are updated with use. This loop is part of the design only and is not built today.

Does the improving system create switching costs or another compounding advantage? Potentially. As answers improve, the system could become a customer self-service and diagnosis tool bundled with our products. That raises loyalty and B2B switching costs, which is the substitutes response set out in C2.

Conclusion Partial/conditional. The proprietary data test is met, but compounding depends on a learning loop that is not yet built, so the moat is potential rather than defensible today.

What would strengthen or erode the moat? Building and running the SME feedback loop, with data governance that keeps recommendation quality high, would strengthen it and could support a move to a customer-facing service. If the loop is never built, or weak governance lets content go stale or leak, P4 is reduced to a copyable content tool.

What result would make you redesign the workflow again: SLA reduction below 20% with a rating below 3/5. Ratings between 3 and 4, or mixed results such as strong SLA gains with a rating below 4, are inconclusive: extend the trial with more cases before deciding.
