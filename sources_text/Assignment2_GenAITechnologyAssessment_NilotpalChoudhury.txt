Why is this the smallest/least costly family that can reliably clear the requirement?

A routed / combination strategy is the smallest reliable option because the workflow contains different task types.
Steps 2 and 4 require strong reasoning over many sources, weak signals and contrarian hypotheses, so limited
frontier-model use is justified there. However, Steps 1, 3 and 5 are mainly structured drafting, summarisation of
supplied outputs, formatting and explanation, which can run on smaller specialised models, templates or
deterministic tools. Quantitative validation itself should remain with dashboard queries, notebooks or traditional
ML; GenAI should explain only supplied results. This design avoids paying frontier-model cost for every iteration
while preserving capability where the strategic judgement load is highest. Enterprise access controls and a model
gateway also keep the model layer swappable if cost, performance or data-control needs change.

Is the gap primarily one of knowledge, behaviour, or both? Explain why your selected approach addresses the actual gap.

The gap is primarily knowledge, with some behaviour control. The system must answer from current public sources,
subscribed/internal CI material and live commercial dashboards, not from model memory. Retrieval is therefore
required to ground each claim in the right evidence and keep analysis current. Behaviour can be managed through
prompts, structured templates, citation rules and human review; fine-tuning is not justified initially because the
model does not need a new capability, only access to the right sources and disciplined output rules.

Autonomy level: [ ] Assisted [ x] Augmented [ ] Autonomous [ ] Not agentic

Why is this the minimum level of autonomy required to capture the value? If not agentic, why is a copilot/assistant or
ordinary automation sufficient?

Augmented is the minimum level that captures the value. The value case is cycle time: a full LT response currently
consumes ~25 analyst-days, most of it in first-pass reading, retrieval and drafting. An assisted copilot would keep
the analyst in the loop for every retrieval and summarisation step, forfeiting most of that saving and leaving little
time for the higher-value work of stress-testing hypotheses and building the storyline. Autonomy above augmented
captures no additional value: the deliverable is an LT recommendation that requires accountable human
judgement regardless, and release is already human-gated, so removing analyst review would add risk without
saving material effort. Augmented lets the agent plan and execute the multi-step research unsupervised within its
read-only source allowlist, while analysts concentrate on validating evidence, challenging the draft
recommendations and shaping the “so what” — the judgement work that justifies the workflow.

Which cost is likely to dominate the economics, and what technology choice could materially reduce it without

The dominant cost is likely to be data engineering and integration, not model inference or repeated ML

tables and pre-computed sensitivities. A routed architecture could reduce cost by using frontier models only for
complex synthesis and challenge, smaller models or templates for routine drafting, and deterministic analytics or
lightweight lookup for quantitative validation.

B2. Hallucination and quality-control design
B2.1 Name the consequential confident error
The most consequential confident error is a false causal link between an external macro/geopolitical event and
business performance. For example, the agent could state that a competitor price move, sanctions change or
demand shock explains a volume or margin movement when the internal dashboard shows a different driver, or
when the cited source does not support that claim. If undetected, the LT could approve the wrong commercial
response, such as changing pricing, inventory posture or customer targeting. Even if analysts catch it, the team
loses time re-checking evidence and confidence in the tool falls. The error is therefore not a generic hallucination;
it is a wrongly evidenced strategic explanation.

No. The architecture deliberately keeps the lethal trifecta broken by allowing sensitive-data access and external-

wider audience. User access should also be role-based, approved by a business owner and reviewed quarterly, but
access management is a supporting control rather than the main trifecta break.

If the current design is not agentic, identify the most consequential action a future team might be tempted to automate
and explain why it should remain outside the model’s authority or behind a human gate.

The likely temptation is to remove the analyst/manager review gate and offer leaders a self-service

Which two protections are non-negotiable for this use case, and why?

The two non-negotiables are data-use/training restrictions and audit/portability/exit rights. Data-use restrictions
are essential because the workflow exposes sensitive LT questions, commercial context, dashboard extracts and
subscribed research material. Audit and exit rights are equally critical because the buyer must be able to
investigate failures, reproduce pilot results and move the workflow if the vendor falls behind, raises prices or no
longer meets policy requirements. Quality / outcome commitments are also a critical go-live condition, even if not
selected as one of the two protections: uptime alone is insufficient for this use case. The contract should tie
remediation to the evaluation harness, including citation accuracy, numerical consistency, omission rate,
evidence-gap behaviour and representative task completion.
