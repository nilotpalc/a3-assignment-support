Part A: Current-State Scan and Readiness
Part A1 – What is Actually Running
Part A1.1 – Inventory of live systems classiﬁed by paradigm and status 1
The internal customer analytics module is a rule-based, production workflow that supports sales teams in
identifying churned and at-risk customers across approximately 50,000 accounts. Its high-level function is to translate
account history into prioritised retention, cross-sell, and upsell actions. The core business value is clear: it has helped
focus sales attention on accounts where intervention could protect or grow margin, contributing double-digit million-
dollar margin growth over three years. However, because the current logic depends on a two-year volume-decline
rule, it is more reactive than predictive.

The internal lead-generation assistant is a generative AI pilot used by marketing teams to research and prioritise
high-potential prospects from event and channel lead lists. Its high-level function is to convert large, relatively
unstructured lead pools — typically 1,000 to 5,000 prospects — into a more focused pipeline for sales follow-up. The
system uses structured prompts and public-source research to assess sector fit and likely buying potential. Its main
advantage is speed and consistency in early-stage prospect research, though the final prioritisation still needs
stronger explainability and governance before production scale-up.

The customer segmentation platform was a classical machine learning workflow that is now retired. It used
promotion-linked consumption data to cluster customers by shared promotional interests, helping marketing teams
identify inactive customers who could be reactivated through targeted campaigns. Its high-level function was to move
beyond broad campaign targeting and create more behaviour-based customer segments. While active, it generated
more than 500 customer activations and approximately low-six-figure USD incremental revenue, showing that
segmentation can create value when the underlying behavioural signal is clean and actionable.

The channel performance dashboard is a hybrid, production system that combines structured transactional data,
external market indicators, forecasting models, fixed allocation logic, and GenAI-assisted explanations. Its high-level
function is to help leadership review channel-level performance and test forward volume and margin positions under
changing market conditions. The dashboard supports quarterly and annual planning by linking historical channel
performance with forward-looking forecasts. Its hybrid nature is important: the forecasting component learns from
data, while the allocation logic remains rule-based and the GenAI layer helps explain performance drivers.

The market intelligence platform is a generative AI, production capability that helps the commercial analysis team
work with external research on markets, customers, competitors, and macro trends. Its high-level function is to
reduce manual research effort by allowing users to query curated intelligence reports and run predefined workflows
for competitor profiling, market monitoring, and strategic reporting. The system is valuable because it converts high-
volume, text-heavy market information into faster and more consistent commercial insight. Its main contribution is not
replacing judgement, but giving analysts a stronger and quicker starting point for strategic interpretation.

Part A1.2 – The AI-vs-Automation test applied honestly to three of them
The AI-versus-automation test shows that the three reviewed workflows should not be treated as uniformly “AI” or
“non-AI”.

For the internal customer analytics module, the current rule-based approach is too late for the business decision
it is meant to support. The existing logic identifies churn or risk only after a sustained decline in volume, by which
point the customer relationship may already have weakened. A more useful answer would change with context:
product-mix shifts, customer feedback, sales-engagement notes, complaint patterns, and promotion response may all
indicate emerging risk before volume loss becomes visible. This makes the opportunity suitable for a supervised AI
model, but only after the underlying account-level data strategy is strengthened. The first move is therefore not

The channel performance dashboard is a hybrid case rather than a pure AI case. Some parts of the workflow,
such as allocation of volume and margin by channel, follow stable business logic and should remain governed
through documented rules, named ownership, and periodic review. Other parts, especially forward forecasting, do
change with the situation because they depend on historical performance and external market indicators. Those
elements justify a learned model, provided that forecasts are monitored against monthly actuals. Where GenAI is
used to explain performance drivers, the control requirement is different again: summaries need spot-checks for
confident but incorrect interpretations before being consumed by the broader sales and marketing community.

The internal lead-generation assistant is also best understood as a hybrid workflow. GenAI is well suited to the
research stage because it has to interpret open-web sources, company context, sector signals, and qualitative
evidence that cannot be fully captured through fixed rules. However, the final likelihood-to-buy ranking should not
depend entirely on GenAI judgement. Since sales teams need to understand and defend why one prospect is
prioritised over another, the final scoring layer should move to written rules over a defined taxonomy of evidence. This
would preserve the value of GenAI in evidence gathering while making the commercial decision more transparent,
auditable, and repeatable.

Part A2.3 – The two weakest ratings analysed, with an internal accountability
factor
Weakest Rating 1

Data readiness (Q2 data Genuinely Ready) for the internal customer analytics module (churn and at-risk
module) is Red, and the reason is a delivery choice made three years ago that has never been revisited: the platform
was built on the easiest available data — sales transactions — with legacy pilot rules to prove the concept quickly,
then embedded into day-to-day working, which removed the urgency to strengthen the foundations. The signals that
would surface risk earlier are not model-ready. Sales engagement minutes are mandated in the CRM but adoption
and quality are a known concern; premium-mix and sectoral drivers are defined differently across countries and
sectors and are recorded nowhere; complaints and escalations reach account managers by email rather than a
controlled incident log, so they never reach a churn-risk score. The accountability factor is internal, not technical —
no decision has been tabled to move beyond transaction-only data, and no data owner has been appointed for the
additional signals 2

Weakest Rating 2

Governance (Q3 Governance outlasts sponsor) on the lead-generation pilot is Red, because it was never
commissioned at all: the pilot began as an ad-hoc business request for GenAI public-web research on prospective
clients, running the whole pipeline through GenAI agents became the default, and no decision was ever tabled to
convert it into a defined capability. This is an internal accountability gap, not a resourcing one. Three consequences
follow — no sector taxonomy exists to structure the evidence, so the likelihood-to-buy rating rests wholly on GenAI
judgement; the prompt instructions, largely transferable across sectors, sit with one responsible data scientist with no
controlled repository, version history, or review forum, so a single people move breaks the service; and because
token costs are low and not recovered internally, there is no financial forcing function for controls. The gap will surface
exactly when it hurts most — a surge in demand or a staff transition, with no documented process to fall back on.

Candidate - Sales teams lack a single, reliable, real-time view of end-to-end order status and inventory (on-ground,
in-transit, back-orders) across SAP/logistics/credit systems, causing manual daily checks, inconsistent customer
updates, missed expectation-setting, and avoidable lost business when supply or lead-times shift. [Regression]
 Ingredient                                                      Present?

 Labelled historical data                                        Partial

 Clear target variable — Delivery Lead Time for a Sales Order    Yes

Two of the three ingredients are Partial, and the constraint is labelling rather than volume. More than five years of
SAP order history sits in the central data warehouse carrying order status, delivery status and actual delivery
outcomes, so promised-versus-actual lead time can be rebuilt at order level and the target variable is unambiguous.
What is missing is explanatory coverage: customer location type, segment, product type, route condition and
manufacturing dependency are not consistently defined or sit outside the current SAP extract, so the model would
learn average lane behaviour rather than exception risk. No named business owner yet validates these fields as
complete, accurate and fit for modelling; appointing one, in logistics or SAP masterdata, is the condition that moves
both Partials to Yes.

Part B1.3 - At least one candidate correctly routed away from AI
The real-time delivery-tracking component of candidate #1 — a live platform continuously predicting lead time
and pushing order-status updates to sales — is routed away from AI; it is a masterdata and process fix. SAP already
holds standard lead times, but they are set from planner judgement with no formal review cycle, so they drift.
Refreshing them periodically from roughly six months of manufacturing and delivery history, seeded offline by the
B1.2 regression, lets the order system quote a realistic date at entry, with threshold rules flagging orders not
scheduled within standard planning time or not dispatched within the lane standard. A live model would add serving,
drift-monitoring and integration cost to restate what refreshed lead times already say, and its scores cannot be
explained to a customer in one sentence — while OTIF is already reasonable and the real pain is the badly delayed
exception. The logistics process owner and the SAP masterdata owner hold this, to be tabled in the next annual
planning cycle with the supply chain organisation.

Part C1.2 – Minimum viable dataset estimated with reasoning 3
The minimum viable dataset is about 5,000 unique accounts per market drawn from central sales history in the
internal data lake, each carrying 12–15 months of continuous transactions plus semantic attributes covering the last
six months of account status — enough month-on-month movement to build stable features, and enough history to
cover a full seasonal cycle. What binds is not row count but the rare event: one market's pilot produced only about
1,500 usable churned accounts, and that is the real training set. Scope therefore stays churn-only, with cross-sell and
up-sell waiting on a wider feature set, and the gap closes by pooling comparable markets into one representative
churn dataset. That needs data-sharing clearance from each market's business data owner, and data governance
council sign-off once the pilot becomes a funded project.

Part C1.3 – Learning or evaluation signal availability4
The signal this model needs is a binary churned / retained flag per account per observation period, and it does not
exist as a field anywhere today — it has to be derived. The source itself is sound: account-level sales history in the
central data lake, fed from the core transaction system, spanning multiple markets, with larger markets holding five-
figure account counts and 5+ years of transactions. Deriving the label is a rule-based build — gaps and sporadic
activity read as zero sales, a sustained absence of orders marks the account churned — costing roughly 2–3 weeks,
about 80–120 hours, of dedicated data engineer or data scientist time, and the churn definition itself needs sign-off
from the business data owner. That capacity is not confirmed: it competes with existing reporting and analytics
commitments, and would have to be secured when the work is formally scheduled and funded.

Part D1.2 – The error-cost trade-oﬀ for the priority use case
The trade-off runs about 5:1 in favour of avoiding false negatives, so the model is tuned toward recall. A missed at-
risk account costs roughly USD 15K — the annual gross margin lost or materially eroded before anyone intervenes
— while a false alarm costs roughly USD 3K in a week of account-manager and customer-operations effort plus an
incentive worth around 10% of gross margin. The probability threshold is therefore set low, accepting more false
alarms so fewer at-risk accounts slip through, and the flags split into two bands: high-probability accounts go to
account managers with the score's top drivers and a required retention conversation; the larger low-probability band
goes to customer operations for lighter-touch outreach. That workload is plausible only if the low band is phased
across the month and capped by queue size — if the queue breaches the agreed cap, the threshold rises or the
rollout is limited by market. 5
